More words. More understanding. More children who can say how they feel.

An illustrated emotions dictionary for primary classrooms, EYFS, SEND settings and family homes.

In almost every consultation I have about a child whose behaviour is worrying someone, I ask the same question.

Does this child have the words for how they feel?

The answer is almost always no. Or: "He knows happy and sad. Maybe angry."

And when we start talking about explicitly teaching and practising emotional vocabulary, something else almost always happens too. The SENCO pauses and says: "Actually, I've got loads of children in my class who would benefit from that."

That moment is the whole problem — and the whole opportunity.

Emotional literacy support too often reaches only the children we're already worried about. But the ability to name, understand, and communicate feelings isn't a specialist skill for struggling children. It's a foundation that every child deserves — built through everyday language, consistent modelling, and the quiet work of adults who are willing to narrate their own emotional experience out loud.

The government's own white paper Every Child Achieving and Thriving (2026) identifies that the UK ranks last out of 27 European countries for how happy 15-year-olds are with their life — and that the number of children who feel they belong at school is declining. By 2029, every school will be expected to monitor children's sense of belonging. Children cannot engage with belonging without the words to understand how they feel.

We're a long way from that being the reality in most schools. But we can start somewhere.

I'm Dr Laura Partington, an Educational Psychologist and former primary school teacher. I've worked in schools across the northeast of England for over fifteen years, supporting children's emotional literacy development through direct work, consultation, and ELSA training. I wrote A-Z of Feelings because I wanted to create something that didn't stay in the therapy room or the SENCO's office. Something that could live in the classroom — available to every child, every day, at the moment they need it.

A-Z of Feelings: A Children's Illustrated Emotions Dictionary

Fifty-two emotion words from amazed to zealous — each paired with a child-friendly definition and an original illustration by Daniela Frongia, whose work captures children in recognisable everyday moments.

Deliberately broad. Deliberately balanced — each letter pairs a positive emotional experience with a harder one, because children feel the full range and deserve words for all of it.

Designed to be explored independently by children seeking a word for how they feel, or used by adults to open conversations about emotional experience.
